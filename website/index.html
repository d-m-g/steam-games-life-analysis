The problem

Big drops are easy to miss until it is too late

Each quarter, publishers decide which live games get updates, sales and marketing. A game about to lose half its audience needs that decision early.

Who it is for

Live-ops and portfolio leads, marketing analysts, storefronts and investors.

Why simple rules fail

  • Player floors miss a drop from 5,000 to 800.
  • Trends confuse post-sale spikes with decline.
  • Year-on-year ignores the market: −26% in 2018, +34% in 2020.

How it works

From player counts to an explained watch list

  1. Collect

    303.6M Steam player counts for 2,000 apps (2017–2020), store data, monthly Twitch stats.

  2. Clean

    1.94M game-days, real games only, Twitch links checked by hand.

  3. Learn

    57 signals from each 90-day window, at 21 monthly start dates.

  4. Score

    A calibrated probability, a tier and three reasons per game.

What counts as a collapse

Players in months 6–12 fall below half of where the typical live game ends up. Only live games (10+ players) are scored.

How it was tested

Retrained each month of 2019 on outcomes known by then, then scored games it had never seen.

      Limits

      What the product does not do

      Big games

      Only 6 of 170 collapsed; the interval (0.46–0.99) is too wide to claim anything.

      New launches

      The trend rule ranks them almost as well (0.75 vs 0.77).

      Causes

      Reasons show which signals raised a score, not why a game declined.

      Scope

      2,000 mostly popular Steam apps, 2017–2020. Safe games are slightly over-predicted.

      Technical report

      Method in brief

      Full details are in the repository (reports/technical_report.md).

      Steam data
      Steam Games Dataset, Wannigamage et al., Mendeley Data, 2020 (CC BY 4.0). 2,000 apps, Dec 2017 – Aug 2020, aggregated to daily means.
      Twitch data
      David Boyd – SullyGnome.com, monthly game statistics 2017–2020, used with permission.
      Cleaning
      1.94M game-days, 1,851 games; 6 wrong Twitch links removed.
      Target
      Players in days 180–365 vs days 0–89, relative to the median live game. Below 0.5 = collapse.
      Training
      21 monthly start dates; each test uses only outcomes known before it.
      Model
      Calibrated random forest (500 trees), compared with logistic regression, boosting and simple rules.
      Explanations
      Exact tree-path decomposition, grouped into 14 themes.

      Learning outcomes

      What we learned building it

      The target defines the product

      "Stays above 10 players" scored 0.98 but only flagged near-empty games.

      Always compare with a baseline

      Every result is set against rules a publisher could apply alone.

      Test the way it will be used

      A monthly backtest showed that more history helps and the market swings.

      Check every join

      A fake "primary genre" and wrong Twitch links changed results until caught.
